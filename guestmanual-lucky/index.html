Getting Comfortable
Your Home at Casa Lucky
🚪

Entry Door

You enter Casa Lucky through the private patio. The metal patio door is secured with an electronic lock — your access code will be provided before arrival. The door locks automatically whenever it closes, so you're always secured.

Once inside, you'll find an additional manual lock on the back of the door — similar to a traditional deadbolt. Use it whenever you want extra privacy inside the condo.

🪟

Interior Sliding Patio Doors

Casa Lucky has two interior sliding glass doors — one in the living room and one in the bedroom — that open onto the patio. Both can be locked from the inside for added privacy. They are not lockable from the exterior, which is perfectly fine — the secure outer entry door protects the entire patio.

Important: Please keep the sliding doors fully closed whenever you leave the condo or whenever the air conditioning is running. Open doors let the cool air escape quickly and put extra strain on the AC unit.

❄️

Air Conditioning

Set the AC to Cooling Mode (snowflake icon on the remote) and keep it above 20°C to prevent the unit from overworking. Please turn the AC off whenever you leave the condo — this helps us keep electricity use sustainable for everyone.

💧

Drinking Water

Casa Lucky has a 3-stage UV water filtration system installed under the kitchen sink. The filtered outlet is right next to the regular faucet. This water is safe and clean to drink. Tip: Fill the provided jug and refrigerate it — cold filtered water is always ready. Fill the ice cube trays in the freezer too! Note: the first second or two from the filtered tap may be warm due to the UV light heating up — just let it run briefly.

📺

TV & Streaming

Smart TVs are available in both the living room and bedroom. Log in with your own streaming accounts — and please remember to log out before you check out.

🔌

Floating Shelf Electronics

The floating shelf in the living room has several electronics that must remain plugged in at all times — please do not unplug them. They power the electronic door lock, the Wi-Fi equipment, and the automatic outdoor watering system. The lights on these devices are normal and expected.

🔒

Safe Box

The safe is located in the bedroom closet. To set your own code: find the small plastic button behind the battery cover, towards the bottom of the door — press it and you'll hear a beep. Enter your chosen code (at least 4 digits) and press E to enter. No second confirmation needed — the safe is now set to your code.

🌐

Internet Reboot

If the Wi-Fi drops, unplug the router, wait 10 seconds, then plug back in. Wait for all lights to stabilize. If it still doesn't work, contact Javier or Osmar.

👕

Laundry

Washer and dryer are available for your use. The laundry area has three sliding doors — keep the door nearest the air discharge filter fully open when running the dryer. The air discharge filter is mounted on the wall nearby and has a tray at the bottom — fill this tray with water before using the dryer for the first time. Clean the dryer lint screen after every load — a clogged screen will stop the machine. Keep both the washer and dryer doors open when not in use to prevent moisture buildup.

🚿

Hot Water & Pressure

Water in Huatulco is stored in rooftop tanks, so what feels like "cold" water may be lukewarm — totally normal! During busy high season, hot water pressure can occasionally dip. Running one shower at a time helps.

♻️

Trash

The outdoor trash receptacles in the complex are labeled Organic, Inorganic, and Recyclable. There is no expectation that you sort trash in the indoor bins — just gather everything and bring it to the appropriate outdoor receptacle at checkout (or whenever the indoor bins are full).

🌿

Plants

Casa Lucky has a few plants that would love a drink once a week while you're here. Much appreciated!

Insider Knowledge
Tips & Tricks
🦶 Foot Wash at the Nirú Gate

Just inside the open double gate to the Nirú Beach Club — on the left side as you're facing the gate — there's a small outdoor foot wash station (available when the gate is open). There's also a wash station at the community pool entrance. Use either on your way back from the beach to rinse sand off your feet before heading up to the condo. Your floors will thank you.

🛍️ Shopping at Chedraui

Mexico charges for plastic bags, so bring a reusable bag when heading to Chedraui. There are a couple in the kitchen drawers at Casa Lucky — feel free to use them and leave them behind for the next guest. If you forget, reusable bags at the store cost just a couple of pesos.

Grocery bagger tip: At checkout, a local bagger will offer to pack your groceries. This is a meaningful community service — they work for tips only. 5–10 pesos is great for a regular shop; 10–20 pesos is appreciated for a full cart. If you prefer to pack yourself, you can politely say:
«No gracias, yo puedo solo/sola.»
(No thank you, I can do it myself.)

🚕 Taxi Tips

Taxis are the easiest way to get around. A ride to La Crucecita or Chedraui typically runs about 50 pesos. No need to tip taxi drivers.

Always ask the fare before getting in:
«¿Cuánto cuesta al [destino]?»
(How much to [destination]?)

Taxi colors matter: Taxis in the area are color-coded by the zone they serve. Make a note of the color of the taxi you take — if you leave something behind, it'll help you track down the right driver.

For guaranteed English-speaking drivers, see the contacts in the Getting Around section below.

🧊 Where to Get Ice

There are several small tienditas near the beach that sell ice, cold beer, alcohol, and snacks. Far easier to grab a bag of ice there than trying to get it back from Chedraui in the heat. Stock up on your way back from the beach!

💳 Adding a Tip at Restaurants

Tipping is similar to back home — 10–15% is standard and appreciated. Some restaurants have card machines with a built-in tip button, but many don't, and your server won't always ask. If you want to add a tip to your card payment, simply say:
«Quisiera agregar propina a la cuenta, por favor.»
(I'd like to add a tip to the bill, please.)

It's also nice to carry smaller peso bills for tips at casual spots, beach vendors, and tour guides.

💰 Pesos at the Beach

Cash is king at the beach. The vendors at Huatulco's bays — fresh coconuts, ceviche, fresh juice, handmade crafts — are all cash-only. But beyond convenience, bringing a little extra to spend with local vendors is one of the most meaningful ways to support the community here. A few pesos goes a long way.

Getting to the Beach

As a Nirú Las Casitas guest, you enjoy a private walkway that takes you directly toward the beach — a route most visitors never see. Here's how to navigate it:

🗺️ Your Private Beach Route

1

Walking from the Nirú Las Casitas parking lot toward the condo, you'll pass the pickleball courts and gym. At that point you'll notice the private walkway veering off to the left — that's your beach route. (When returning to the condo from the parking lot, continue to the right to head up to the unit.) The walkway runs past the Nirú Beach Club.

2

The walkway exits at the Nirú Beach Club entrance gate. The key left in the condo opens this locked door.

3

After exiting, close and lock the door behind you.

4

Turn right, walk under the tree leaning over the sidewalk, and follow the "Playa" letters painted on the building that houses the Doña Celia restaurant.

5

On your way back, use the foot wash station on the left just inside the gate to rinse the sand off before heading up.
